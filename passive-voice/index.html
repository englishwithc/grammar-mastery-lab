Step 4 of 8

The Great Transformation

This is the heart of it. A passive sentence is built from an active one in three moves. Watch it happen — then drive it yourself.

▲ old object — now the new subject ▲ the verb splits in two ▲ old subject — now the agent

The recipe (memorise this)

OBJECT + be + PAST PARTICIPLE + (+ by …)
  • The old object becomes the new subject — it moves to the front.
  • The verb becomes be + past participle (wrote → was written).
  • The old subject becomes the agent, after by — and pronouns change form (he → him).

Step 6 of 8

"By X" … or not "by X"?

The passive lets you leave out the agent — the person who did it. In fact, most passive sentences in real life have no "by" phrase at all. But when must you keep it? Be the editor and decide.

Three good reasons to drop "by X"

  • Unknown — we don't know who did it. My wallet was stolen.
  • Obvious — everyone can guess. You will be asked to show your ID (by test-centre staff).
  • Unimportant — the action or result is what matters. The streets are cleaned every morning.

Keep it only when the agent is new and important information — often a name, or a surprising performer of the action.

Why we use the passive

Choosing the passive is a choice about what's important. Active puts the doer in front. Passive puts the action — and the receiver — in front. Read the context, choose the voice.

The Passive Voice — Cheat Sheet

Formula: OBJECT + be + PAST PARTICIPLE (+ by …)

How to transform (3 moves)

  • The object moves to the front and becomes the new subject.
  • The verb becomes be + past participle (the tense lives in be).
  • The old subject goes to the end after by (pronouns change: I→me, he→him). Often omitted!

Be forms by tense

  • Present simple: am / is / are  ·  Present continuous: am/is/are being
  • Past simple: was / were  ·  Past continuous: was/were being
  • Present perfect: have/has been  ·  Past perfect: had been
  • will: will be  ·  going to: am/is/are going to be  ·  modals: must/can/may be
  • Infinitive: (to) be  ·  -ing form: being  ·  used to: used to be

Drop or keep "by X"?

  • Drop: agent unknown, obvious, or unimportant.
  • Keep: agent is new, important information (e.g. Hamlet was written by Shakespeare).

When do we use the passive?

  • The action/object matters more than the doer.
  • Formal, impersonal writing (impersonal it: It is argued that…).
  • Describing (human) processes — natural processes usually stay active.

Remember

  • Only verbs with an object can go passive. Voice is not a tense — it's a different way of presenting the same event.
